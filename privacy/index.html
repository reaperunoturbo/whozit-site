Privacy policy

How the current WhoZit Android app uses caller information and data stored on your phone.

1. About this policy

Effective date: 19 September 2026
Responsible party: Johan du Preez (publisher of WhoZit)
Address: 18 Fir Street, Virginia, Free State, South Africa
Privacy contact: whozitinfo@gmail.com

This policy explains how WhoZit accesses and uses information when you use the WhoZit Android app. WhoZit provides caller identification, call screening, number search, business-profile screens, local spam reporting, and number blocking. The current beta has no public caller or verified-business directory records. The app does not require an account or operate a WhoZit server that receives your contacts, calls, searches, reports, blocked numbers, saved businesses, or notes.

2. Information WhoZit accesses or stores

Phone numbers and incoming calls

When Android sends an eligible incoming call to WhoZit's call-screening service, WhoZit checks the number against its packaged directory, your local spam reports, and your local blocklist. It may store the number, caller label, event type, explanation, and time in local call activity. The app keeps up to 100 recent call-activity entries.

Contacts

If you grant Contacts permission, WhoZit asks Android whether a particular incoming or searched number matches a contact saved on your phone. This helps it show a saved name and avoid labelling an ordinary contact as unknown. A resolved contact name may appear in local call activity. WhoZit does not upload your address book or make your contacts available to other WhoZit users.

Searches and caller controls

WhoZit stores recent number and business searches, spam reports and reasons, blocked numbers, saved businesses, and personal caller notes locally on your phone. Notes are limited to 160 characters per caller.

Beta and diagnostic information

The app can display checklist progress and device details such as manufacturer, model, Android version, permission status, and local-data counts. It does not automatically transmit this information. You choose whether to copy or share a report using Android's sharing tools.

Packaged directory

WhoZit has a directory packaged with the app, but the current beta's directory is empty. It contains no public caller or verified-business records. The packaged directory is not taken from your phone.

3. Why information is used

WhoZit uses information to provide the features you request or enable: identifying eligible incoming callers, distinguishing saved contacts from unknown callers, warning about locally reported numbers, blocking numbers you choose, showing activity and search results, and storing favourites and private notes. Device information helps diagnose setup or compatibility problems when you choose to view or share it.

WhoZit limits processing to what is needed for these functions. Where consent is required, it relies on the permission or choice you make. Other processing needed to provide caller identification and call protection is performed to provide the service and protect users' legitimate interests, subject to applicable law.

4. Storage, transmission, and sharing

User-created information is kept in private app storage on your Android device. Android backup is disabled for WhoZit. WhoZit does not sell personal information or automatically send locally stored data to WhoZit or an advertising, analytics, or data-broker service.

Some actions you choose open another app or service, such as the Phone app, a browser, maps, Contacts, or Android's sharing tools. The receiving app or service handles that information under its own privacy terms.

5. Retention and deletion

Locally stored information remains on your device until you remove it, clear WhoZit data, or uninstall the app. In Settings → Privacy, you can clear search history or all local WhoZit data, including call activity, reports, blocked numbers, saved businesses, and notes. Checklist progress can be reset separately. Packaged directory data changes when the app is updated or removed.

6. Security

WhoZit uses Android private app storage and Android's permission and call-screening systems. Release packages are digitally signed. Reasonable safeguards are maintained and reviewed, but no device or software system can be guaranteed completely secure. If a security compromise involving personal information occurs, the responsible party will assess and make notifications required by South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 (POPIA).

7. Your rights and directory corrections

Subject to POPIA, you may ask whether the responsible party holds personal information about you and request access, correction, deletion, or object to processing where applicable. If a directory entry is inaccurate, misleading, outdated, or should be removed, email whozitinfo@gmail.com with enough detail to identify the entry. Do not send identity documents unless specifically and securely requested. You may also complain to the Information Regulator (South Africa).

8. Children's information

WhoZit is not designed to collect information from children and does not provide accounts or social features for children. If you believe a child's information appears in the directory, contact the privacy address above promptly.

9. Changes to this policy

This policy will be updated if WhoZit's information practices change. The effective date above will identify the current version. Material changes will be presented through the app or its store listing where appropriate.

10. Contact

Send privacy and POPIA enquiries to whozitinfo@gmail.com or the responsible party at the address above.
